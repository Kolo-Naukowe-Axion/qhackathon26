Quantum annealing for a QUBO, explained

Part 3 of the qhackathon26 method guides. Parts 1 and 2 built the QUBO (factory tutorial, kitchen QUBO (not yet in docs/)) and solved it with circuits (QAOA, VQE, kitchen VQE). Quantum annealing solves the same QUBO with no gates at all: the qubits are placed in a simple energy landscape and the landscape is slowly deformed into the one defined by Q. Every number on this page comes from an exact simulation; the figures are static, the boxes marked Try it are live.

The one idea of this page. A quantum system left alone settles into its lowest-energy state. If the energy is the QUBO E(x), that state is the best schedule. We cannot prepare it directly, but we can prepare the lowest-energy state of a simple energy HM (every arrow equal, the QAOA start state) and then change the energy slowly from HM to HC. The adiabatic theorem says that if the change is slow enough, the system stays in the lowest-energy state the whole way and ends in the answer. "Slow enough" is set by the smallest energy gap met on the way, and that gap is where everything interesting happens.

3. From the QUBO to the two Hamiltonians

3.1 The cost Hamiltonian HC (recap of tutorial §6)

General

Substitute xi=1−zi2 into E(x)=∑iQiixi+∑i<jQijxixj+c and collect:

E=c′+∑ihizi+∑i<jJijzizj
hi=−Qii2−14∑j≠iQij,Jij=Qij4

Replacing each spin zi by the operator Zi gives the cost Hamiltonian:

HC=c′I+∑ihiZi+∑i<jJijZiZj

It is diagonal: on the bitstring |x⟩ every Zi is just the number zi, so HC|x⟩=E(x)|x⟩. The fields hi and couplings Jij are exactly the numbers sent to an annealer. The constant c′ shifts all levels equally and is dropped.

Example: Instance A, H = 4, domain wall (4 bits)
Q=[0−1100−101001−10000],c=2
h=(0, 0.5, −0.5, 0),c′=2
J12=−0.25, J13=0.25, J24=0.25, J34=−0.25
HC=2I+0.5Z2−0.5Z3−0.25Z1Z2+0.25Z1Z3+0.25Z2Z4−0.25Z3Z4

Check on the best schedule 1100, spins (−1,−1,+1,+1): 2−0.5−0.5−0.25−0.25−0.25−0.25=0 ✓. Only 1100 has E=0; the 16 energies are 2, 2, 3, 2, 1, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 4, 3, 0, 1, 2, 2 (in the order 0000 … 1111).

3.2 The driver HM: a simple landscape whose ground state we can prepare

General

For one qubit, −X has the two eigenvectors

−X|+⟩=−|+⟩,−X|−⟩=+|−⟩,|±⟩=|0⟩±|1⟩2

because X swaps the two amplitudes: X(1,1)=(1,1) and X(1,−1)=(−1,1)=−(1,−1). So the ground state of −X is |+⟩ with energy −1. For n qubits

HM=−∑i=1nXi,HM|+⟩⊗n=−n|+⟩⊗n

The ground state |+⟩⊗n has every amplitude equal to 2−n/2: all arrows the same, the QAOA start state. Physically it is what a strong magnetic field along x (a transverse field) does to spins, so it is easy to prepare: switch the field on and wait.

HM is not diagonal. Its off-diagonal entries connect every bitstring to its one-bit-flip neighbours, exactly the pairs the QAOA mixer added together. That is why it can move probability between bitstrings and HC cannot.

Example: two qubits, order 00, 01, 10, 11
HM=−X1−X2=−[0110100110010110]

Row 00 has entries in columns 01 and 10 (its neighbours) and 0 in column 11 (two bits differ). The equal state 12(1,1,1,1) is an eigenvector with eigenvalue −2: each row sums to −2.

For the two-job toy problem (E=1,0,0,1), the cost Hamiltonian is HC=12I+12Z1Z2: substitute x=(1−z)/2 into 1−x1−x2+2x1x2 and every linear term cancels.

4. The schedule: sliding from the driver to the cost
General
H(s)=A(s)HM+B(s)HC,s=tT∈[0,1]

with A(0)≫B(0) (driver dominates at the start) and A(1)≪B(1) (cost dominates at the end). The procedure:

  1. CPU compute hi,Jij from Q; scale them into the chip's allowed range; send them.
  2. QPU prepare |+⟩⊗n, the ground state of H(0).
  3. QPU let the qubits evolve under H(t/T) for a time T. Nothing is measured, no gates are applied; the control electronics only change the field strengths A,B.
  4. QPU at t=T measure every qubit: one bitstring.
  5. CPU repeat steps 2–4 many times (reads); decode, check and keep the best valid bitstring.
Example: the linear schedule used on this page
A(s)=1−s,B(s)=s
H(s)=−(1−s)∑iXi+sHC

At s=0: pure driver, ground state |+⟩⊗n, energy −n. At s=1: pure cost, ground state the best schedule, energy Emin. In between the ground state is a mixture that we can compute but the chip never shows us.

Real devices use curved A(s),B(s) in units of GHz and let the user stretch or pause parts of the schedule; the physics is the same.

The whole method rests on one question: does the actual state |ψ(t)⟩ stay in the ground state of H(s) while s moves? If yes, the final measurement returns the best schedule with probability 1. Section 5 shows a case where we can answer by hand; section 6 gives the general rule.

5. One qubit by hand

Toy problem: one bit, E(0)=0 (good) and E(1)=1 (bad), so E=x=1−z2 and HC=12I−12Z, i.e. h=−12.

Write α=−rcosθ, β=−rsinθ. Then the ground state (eigenvalue −r) is

6. The adiabatic theorem: how slow is slow enough?
Statement

Start in the ground state |0(0)⟩ of H(0) and change H over a time T. If

T≫maxs|⟨1(s)|dHds|0(s)⟩|Δmin2

then the state stays close to the instantaneous ground state |0(s)⟩ for all s, and at the end it is the ground state of HC: the best schedule.

The numerator is how strongly the change of H connects the ground state to the first excited state; for the linear schedule dH/ds=HC−HM, a fixed matrix. The denominator is what varies from problem to problem: the required time grows like 1/Δmin2.

Derivation sketch (why 1/Δ2)

Expand the state in the instantaneous eigenstates, with their phases pulled out:

|ψ(t)⟩=∑kck(t)e−iϕk(t)|k(s)⟩,ϕk(t)=∫0tEkdt′

Insert into the Schrödinger equation and project onto ⟨k|; the Ek terms cancel against the phases, leaving only the motion of the eigenvectors:

c˙k=−∑mcm⟨k|m˙⟩ei(ϕk−ϕm)

Differentiate H|m⟩=Em|m⟩ and project onto ⟨k| (k≠m): ⟨k|H˙|m⟩=(Em−Ek)⟨k|m˙⟩, so

⟨k|m˙⟩=⟨k|H˙|m⟩Em−Ek=1T⟨k|dHds|m⟩Em−Ek

Leakage out of the ground state (m=0, k=1) is therefore driven by a term of size 1T|⟨1|dH/ds|0⟩|Δ, multiplied by a phase ei(ϕ1−ϕ0) that rotates at rate Δ. A rotating term averages away; integrating it contributes at most its size divided by its rate, another factor 1/Δ. The leaked amplitude is small when |⟨1|dH/ds|0⟩|TΔ2≪1, which is the statement on the left. (Making the last step rigorous is what the full proofs do.)

6.1 The exactly solvable case: Landau–Zener

General

Near a small gap only two levels matter. Model them as two straight lines that would cross at speed v, coupled by Δ/2:

H(t)=[vt/2Δ/2Δ/2−vt/2],E±(t)=±12v2t2+Δ2

The coupling turns the crossing into an avoided crossing with minimum gap Δ. Starting in the lower level long before the crossing, the probability of ending in the upper level long after it is (Landau, Zener, 1932)

Pjump=exp(−πΔ22v)

For a linear schedule the speed is v=κ/T with κ the slope of the two crossing levels in s, so Pjump=e−πΔ2T/2κ: the error falls exponentially with T, and the time for a fixed error grows as 1/Δ2, as the theorem said.

7. Two jobs: a degenerate answer

The two-job toy (one machine, A and B, H=2) has two best schedules, 01 and 10. HC=12I+12Z1Z2 gives them both energy 0 and the clashes energy 1. Does an anneal get confused by a tie?

  • H(s) is unchanged if qubits 1 and 2 are swapped, for every s. The start state |++⟩ is symmetric under the swap, so the state stays symmetric for ever: it can never acquire a component on the antisymmetric level (dashed), even where that level comes close.
  • At s=1 the symmetric ground state is (|01⟩+|10⟩)/2: the same entangled state the VQE page produced with a CNOT. Measuring it gives 01 or 10 with probability 12 each, never a clash.
  • A tie between equally good answers is therefore harmless: the anneal lands in a superposition of them and the measurement picks one. What matters is the gap to the states that are worse.

8. Instance A: 16 levels, one gap, one knob

The real 2×2 baseline (section 3.1): 4 qubits, 16 levels. The spectrum of H(s) can be computed exactly by diagonalising a 16×16 matrix at each s.

General: what the gap predicts

The theorem gives a scale, not a sharp threshold: the anneal starts to succeed when T is a few times 1/Δmin2, and the remaining error then falls off quickly (exponentially in the Landau–Zener picture). With a fast anneal the state is simply the start state: random guessing, P=1/2n.

9. QAOA is a chopped anneal
General: the split step

Over a short time Δt with s almost constant, the evolution is e−iΔtH(s). For two matrices that do not commute, eΔt(A+B)=eΔtAeΔtB+O(Δt2) (Trotter). Applied to H(s)=sHC+(1−s)HM:

e−iΔtH(s)≈e−isΔtHCe−i(1−s)ΔtHM

The first factor is the QAOA cost layer with γ=sΔt. The second is e+i(1−s)Δt∑Xi, a product of RX rotations: the QAOA mixer with β=−(1−s)Δt. Cutting the anneal into p pieces gives a p-layer QAOA circuit with angles fixed by the schedule:

γk=skTp,βk=−(1−sk)Tp,sk=k−12p

QAOA then goes one step further: it treats the 2p angles as free and tunes them, which lets a few layers do what the schedule needs many steps for.

Example: Instance A, T = 20
pieces p5102050200exact anneal
P(1100)0.0330.5260.9940.9950.9950.995

With 20 or more pieces the chopped version reproduces the anneal. With 5 pieces each step is too coarse (Δt=4) and the approximation fails. For comparison, QAOA with only 3 layers but tuned angles reached 0.958.

So the two pictures are one: the QAOA page's "tag the arrows, then mix" is the anneal's "cost and driver on at once", performed in alternating slices.

10. The kitchen: the penalty sets the gap, and the chip has finite precision

Now the 9-bit kitchen QUBO of Part 1, section 9 (groups a, b, c with two pots and an idle bit each; penalty P=11141287; best plan a0·b1·c-idle with energy 68). An annealer receives its Ising form, and two things about that form matter that did not matter to the VQE.

10.1 Everything is scaled to the chip's range

General

A chip accepts fields and couplings only inside fixed ranges, so the whole problem is divided by one number λ so that the largest |hi| or |Jij| fits. Dividing HC by λ does not change which bitstring is best, but it divides every energy difference, and every gap, by λ. The time needed then grows by λ2.

Programmed values also carry noise of roughly a per cent of the range. A coefficient much smaller than that is effectively not there.

10.2 The penalty sets the gap

General

The penalty must exceed the largest score difference between valid plans so that no rule-breaking plan can win. Anything above that only inflates λ and compresses the scores. The gap near the end of the anneal is set by the score difference between the best and second-best valid plan, divided by λ; a penalty 100× larger than necessary makes that gap 100× smaller and the required time 10 000× longer.

With the repository's penalty the most likely outcome even at T=1500 is a-idle·b-idle·c-idle ("cook nothing", 35 %): the anneal only resolves the penalties and spreads over the valid plans almost blindly.

Rule for annealers. Choose the smallest penalty that still makes every invalid plan lose, and keep the score terms as large as the rules allow. The VQE of Part 2 avoided this problem by building "one choice per group" into its circuit; an annealer has no such circuit, so the penalties must be in HC and their size is a real design decision.

11. Real hardware

11.1 What a D-Wave annealer is

  • Superconducting loops at about 15 mK act as the spins; programmable currents set the fields hi and couplings Jij. The current generation, Advantage2, has more than 4 400 qubits and over 40 000 couplers in the Zephyr layout, where each qubit is coupled to 20 others. A standard anneal lasts between 0.5 µs and 2 000 µs; a "fast anneal" mode goes down to 5 ns.
  • One anneal returns one bitstring. A job is typically 100–10 000 anneals ("reads"); the result is a list of distinct bitstrings with their energies and how often each appeared. The CPU then decodes and validates them exactly as in sections 8–11 of the QAOA page.
  • The device is not isolated or at zero temperature. Thermal excitation and noise mean the returned samples resemble a low-temperature statistical mixture rather than the pure ground state, even for slow anneals. Repeated reads and "take the best valid sample" are part of the method, not a workaround.

11.2 Minor embedding: when Q has more neighbours than the chip

General

A coupling Jij can only be programmed between physically connected qubits. If bit i interacts with more bits than a qubit has couplers (20 on Zephyr), it is represented by a chain of several physical qubits tied together by a strong ferromagnetic coupling −Jchain that makes them agree. The chain strength is one more number to set: too weak and chains "break" (qubits of one bit disagree, the read is discarded or repaired by majority vote); too strong and it inflates λ like a large penalty does. Finding the chains is a classical graph problem (minorminer in D-Wave's tools).

Example

Instance A (4 bits, 4 couplings) and the 9-bit kitchen (every bit couples to at most 8 others) fit directly, no chains. The full 35-bit kitchen has 520 couplings; a pot in the 19-bit group c couples to all 18 other bits of its group plus every clashing pot elsewhere, more than 20, so chains are needed there.

13. What is real on this page, and what is not
  • All levels, probabilities and anneals are exact numerical results for the ideal closed-system model H(s) with a linear schedule, at zero temperature, with no noise. Real annealers are warmer, noisier and use curved schedules; the qualitative conclusions (gap sets time, penalty sets gap, precision is finite) carry over, the numbers do not.
  • Nothing was run on an annealer. The team's hackathon work used Qiskit simulations (QAOA), classical simulated annealing from dwave-samplers, and VeloxQ, a classical solver.
  • Browser widgets integrate the Schrödinger equation with a split-step method (step 0.02); they agree with the Python results to three decimals.
Check yourself (answers hidden)

1. Why does the anneal start in |+⟩⊗n? It is the ground state of the driver HM, which is what H(0) is.

2. The gap of a problem is halved. How much longer must the anneal be? About four times: T∝1/Δ2.

3. Two schedules tie for the optimum. Is that a problem? No: the anneal ends in a superposition of them and the measurement picks one (section 7).

4. Why is a huge penalty harmful for an annealer but harmless for brute force? Scaling to the chip's range shrinks every score difference and gap by the same factor; brute force compares exact numbers.

5. What does QAOA with γk=skT/p, βk=−(1−sk)T/p compute? A Trotter approximation of the anneal of length T in p steps (section 9).

14. Glossary
adiabatic
slow enough that the system stays in its instantaneous ground state.
anneal time T
the duration of the sweep from driver to cost.
avoided crossing
two levels that would cross but repel because of a coupling; the point of smallest gap.
chain, chain strength
several physical qubits representing one bit; the coupling that keeps them equal.
driver / transverse field HM
−∑Xi; moves amplitude between neighbouring bitstrings.
gap Δ(s)
E1(s)−E0(s); its minimum decides the required anneal time.
ground state
the eigenvector of lowest eigenvalue; for HC, the best schedule.
Landau–Zener formula
Pjump=e−πΔ2/2v for a linear two-level crossing.
minor embedding
mapping the problem's coupling graph onto the chip's physical graph using chains.
read
one anneal plus one measurement; returns one bitstring.
schedule A(s),B(s)
the time profiles of the driver and cost strengths.
Trotter step
eΔt(A+B)≈eΔtAeΔtB; the link between annealing and QAOA.
