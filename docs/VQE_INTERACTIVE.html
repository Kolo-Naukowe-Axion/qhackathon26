1Same problem, different circuit

Everything you built for QAOA still holds: the state is a list of 2n arrows; measuring gives one bitstring with probability |cx|2; gates rewrite the list; the CPU scores the measured bitstrings by their energy E(x) and an optimiser tunes the angles. The only question is which circuit makes the arrows.

VQE in one sentence. Pick a circuit with adjustable angles, measure the energy of what it produces, and let an ordinary optimiser turn the angles until the average energy is as low as possible. (Variational Quantum Eigensolver, Peruzzo et al. 2014; it was invented for chemistry, where the energy is not a simple function of bitstrings. For a QUBO it is, which makes everything below simpler.)

2Why minimising the average works

The average energy of the measured bitstrings is a weighted average of the energies, with the probabilities as weights:

⟨E⟩=∑xP(x)E(x)≥∑xP(x)Emin=Emin

Since E(x)≥Emin for every x and the probabilities add up to 1, the average can never go below the lowest energy. It equals Emin only if all probability sits on lowest-energy bitstrings. So "make the average as small as possible" automatically means "put the probability on the best schedules". This is the variational principle.

Each slider sets a weight; the weights are rescaled to probabilities. Try to push ⟨E⟩ below 0.

For a QUBO this is all there is: the energy is diagonal (each bitstring has one fixed energy), so measuring every qubit in the usual 0/1 basis is enough to estimate ⟨E⟩. In chemistry the energy has non-diagonal parts and needs several kinds of measurement; we do not.

3The adjustable gate: RY(θ)

The most common VQE gate is again a dial between "keep" and "flip", but with ordinary real numbers instead of the −i of the QAOA mixer:

RY(θ)=[cosθ2−sinθ2sinθ2cosθ2]
RY(θ)[10]=[cosθ2sinθ2],P(1)=sin2θ2
  • Why is it a legal gate? It is an ordinary rotation of the pair (c0,c1) in the plane, so the length c02+c12 (the total probability) never changes. The QAOA mixer needed −i to achieve the same thing; here the minus sign in the corner does it.
  • Why θ/2? Convention: θ=π is a full flip |0⟩→|1⟩, and θ=2π brings you back to −|0⟩ (same probabilities).
  • What it does to the arrows: all amplitudes stay real, so every arrow points right or left (positive or negative), never up or down. Phases play almost no role in this kind of VQE.

4Two qubits without entanglement: a landscape you can see

The simplest ansatz: one RY per qubit, starting from |00⟩. Each qubit is set independently, so the probabilities multiply. Write pA=sin2(θA/2) for "A is measured as 1" (same for B):

P(01)=(1−pA)pB,P(11)=pApB,…

Put this into ⟨E⟩=∑xP(x)E(x) for the toy energy E=1−yA−yB+2yAyB and you get the same formula with each bit replaced by its probability:

⟨E⟩=1−pA−pB+2pApB

In general, for independent qubits ⟨E⟩=c+∑iQiipi+∑i<jQijpipj. That is the QUBO with "0 or 1" relaxed to "any probability between 0 and 1".

Try it · the landscape, and gradient descent on it
  • The two dark valleys at (θA,θB)=(0,π) and (π,0) are the two valid schedules 01 and 10, each with probability 1.
  • The centre (π/2,π/2) is a saddle with ⟨E⟩=0.5: downhill in one direction, uphill in the other.
  • Start at (1.0, 1.0): both qubits start identical, so they get identical gradients and stay identical. The path slides along the diagonal into the saddle and stops. Start at (1.0, 1.2): the small difference grows and the path rolls into a valley. Tick shot noise and the symmetric start escapes too: the noise breaks the tie.

How the gradient is measured on a quantum chip

As a function of any one angle, ⟨E⟩ always has the form a+bcosθ+csinθ: the amplitudes contain cosθ2 and sinθ2, and probabilities are their squares and products, e.g. cos2θ2=1+cosθ2. For such a function the slope is exactly

d⟨E⟩dθ=⟨E⟩(θ+π2)−⟨E⟩(θ−π2)2

This is the parameter-shift rule: two extra circuit runs per angle give the exact slope, not an approximation. The gradient-descent buttons above use it (4 circuit runs per step for 2 angles).

An honest warning. Without entanglement every qubit is independent, so ⟨E⟩ is just the relaxed formula above, which a laptop evaluates instantly for thousands of bits. Product-state VQE is therefore a classical method in disguise. It is a useful baseline and warm start, but it cannot give a quantum advantage.

5Entanglement: the CNOT gate

To make qubits depend on each other we need a two-qubit gate. The CNOT ("controlled NOT") flips B if A is 1. On the list of four arrows it simply swaps the arrows of 10 and 11:

CNOT=[1000010000010010],[c00c01c10c11]⟶[c00c01c11c10]
Try it · RY, RY, then CNOT

With (θA,θB)=(π/2,π) the two RY gates give (|01⟩+|11⟩)/2; the CNOT turns the clash |11⟩ into |10⟩:

12(|01⟩+|11⟩)⟶CNOT12(|01⟩+|10⟩)

Now every shot is a valid schedule (⟨E⟩=0), yet neither job's start time is decided: whatever A does, B does the opposite. That correlation is entanglement. Without the CNOT it is impossible: independent qubits with P(00)=(1−pA)(1−pB)=0 and P(11)=pApB=0 force pA or pB to be exactly 0 or 1, and then one of the two schedules has probability 0.

Do we need entanglement for a QUBO? Not to represent the answer: the best schedule is one bitstring, which the product ansatz reaches exactly (θi∈{0,π}). Entanglement changes how the optimiser can get there (more routes through the landscape) and lets the state hold several good schedules at once. In an exact test on Instance A (50 random starts, Nelder–Mead), the product ansatz reached the optimum from 48/50 starts, one entangling layer from 50/50.

6The full ansatz: layers of RY and CNOT

The standard hardware-efficient ansatz repeats a layer of RY gates (one angle per qubit) and a chain of CNOTs between neighbouring qubits, and ends with one more RY layer. With n qubits and r entangling layers:

m=n(r+1) angles,r(n−1) CNOTs
The circuit for Instance A (4 qubits)
  • "Hardware-efficient" means it uses only gates the chip does well (single-qubit rotations, CNOTs between neighbours) and stays shallow. Its size depends on n and r, not on Q: the same circuit is used for every problem with n bits, however many terms Q has.
  • The price: nothing in the circuit points it toward good schedules; only the optimiser does. Deep circuits of this kind become very hard to train because the landscape turns flat almost everywhere ("barren plateaus", McClean et al. 2018). Keep r small.

7Scoring the shots: mean energy or CVaR

The score is computed on the CPU from the measured bitstrings. The obvious choice is the mean energy of all S shots. For optimisation there is a better one: we only need the best bitstring to show up, not the average to be perfect. CVaR (conditional value at risk) averages only the best fraction α of the shots:

CVaRα=mean of the ⌈αS⌉ lowest shot energies

α=1 is the plain mean; small α rewards circuits that hit the optimum sometimes (Barkoutsos et al. 2020).

Try it · the same shots, two scores

Instance A, 1000 shots. The slider t moves the four qubits from random guessing (t=0, all θ=π/2) to exactly 1100 (t=1). Bright bars are the shots CVaR counts.

At random guessing the mean is about 2, but CVaR0.1≈0.3 because 6 % of the shots are already the optimum. A consequence you will see in section 8: training with CVaR0.25 stops once roughly a quarter of the shots hit 1100, since that is all the score asks for. The best bitstring is still found; the state is just less concentrated.

8The VQE loop on Instance A

stepwherewhat happens
0CPUwrite the problem as E(x)=x⊤Qx+c (Instance A, H=4, 4 bits)
1CPUchoose the ansatz (r layers, m=n(r+1) angles) and random starting angles
2QPUrun the circuit from |0000⟩ and measure; repeat S times. Only bitstrings come back
3CPUscore: E of each bitstring, then the mean (or CVaR)
4CPUthe optimiser proposes new angles; back to step 2 until the budget is used
5CPUanswer: the lowest-energy bitstring among the final shots, decoded and checked
Try it · VQE on Instance A

Optimiser: Nelder–Mead on the noisy shot scores, budget 60 tries per angle. "New random start" changes the starting angles; some starts end in a worse valley, which is why real runs use several restarts.

In a batch test of this exact widget set-up (20 random starts, 512 shots per try, mean score), P(1100) ended above 0.9 for 19/20 starts with the product ansatz, 19/20 with one entangling layer and 20/20 with two (medians 0.996, 0.987, 0.991). With CVaR0.25 the medians ended at 0.31–0.33, as explained in section 7, and 1100 was still the lowest-energy bitstring sampled in all 60 runs.

Read the last row carefully. On a 4-qubit problem whose answer is one bitstring, VQE reaching probability ≈ 1 says little about quantum usefulness: the product ansatz alone, which is classically simulable, already does it. A fair Stage 2 comparison would fix the shot budget, use several seeds, include larger instances, and compare against the classical relaxation and exact solvers.

10What is real on this page, and what is not

  • All arrows, probabilities and shots come from an exact state-vector simulation in your browser, with seeded pseudo-random shots. Nothing ran on quantum hardware.
  • The repository does not contain a VQE solver yet; VQE appears in CONTEXT.md as one of the suggested Stage 2 comparisons. The numbers here were cross-checked against an independent Python simulation.
  • The optimiser here is Nelder–Mead (derivative-free) plus a parameter-shift gradient-descent demo; the repository's QAOA code uses COBYLA.
Check yourself (answers hidden)

1. Where does Q enter a VQE run? Only in the score: the CPU computes E(x) for the measured bitstrings.

2. Can ⟨E⟩ ever be below the lowest energy? No: it is a weighted average of energies (variational principle).

3. Why does gradient descent from (1.0,1.0) stop at the saddle? Both qubits get identical gradients, so they stay identical and slide into the symmetric saddle point.

4. Why can the product ansatz still find the optimal schedule? The optimum is a single bitstring, i.e. each qubit at 0 or 1: no entanglement needed to represent it.

5. Why does CVaR training leave P(optimum) near α? The score only looks at the best α fraction of shots; once those are all optimal, the score cannot improve further.
