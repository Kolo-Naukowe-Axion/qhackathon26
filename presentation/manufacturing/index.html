How the schedule becomes a QUBO

01 / A DECISION BIT

Choose an assignment.

One binary x selects an operation, eligible machine, qualified worker and start. Exactly one assignment is required for each operation. A separate one-hot group chooses the completion bound.

02 / THE COST

Prioritize deadlines, then completion.

J = (H + 1) · weighted late minutes
+ chosen completion bound

Here all times and weights are integers and completion is at most H. One fewer weighted late minute outweighs any completion-time change.

03 / THE QUADRATIC ENERGY

E = J + P ∑groups (∑ z − 1)²
+ P ∑forbidden pairs i<j nij zizj

z includes assignment and completion bits. Forbidden pairs cover resource clashes, setup/travel separation, precedence and finishing beyond the chosen bound. n counts violated pair reasons and is 1 for completion-consistency pairs; each upper-triangle pair appears once. No extra bits are needed to square a binary one-hot sum.

A checked feasible witness represented by this QUBO, using its minimum completion register, has objective U and permits P = U + 1: an invalid encoding pays at least P, while that witness costs U. This uses the nonnegative objective and integer constraint penalties. Exact pruning, a restricted candidate pool and the hard-deadline query have different guarantees.

This is the assignment optimization formulation. The 818-bit feasibility query omits the completion register and soft objective; the quantum bridge chooses restricted complete component routes.

Model boundaries and provenance

This synthetic planning model retains every housing, rotor, flange, assembly and final inspection operation. It includes alternative machines, skilled worker calendars, attendance, inter-hall worker travel, minimum component transfer lags, machine-family changeovers and terminal due dates. It excludes capacitated vehicles, finite buffers, energy, tools, stochastic quality, rework and shipment. Those remain in the separately linked physical factory. Outages here withdraw a machine for the whole window; no running operation is interrupted.

Playback shows the selected retained native-reference or QAOA schedule. The fresh QAOA action requests a local Python simulation; no remote solver is called. Native CP OPTIMAL is distinguished from FEASIBLE and UNKNOWN. A restricted-pool optimum is not a whole-model certificate. The independent lower bound relaxes shared resource contention. Development scenarios were selected after exploratory probes; this is not a held-out final campaign.





UNDERSTAND THE RESULT
What the schedules tell us.
The main finding is the operational cost of discarding scheduling choices. These answers keep the model, the solver and the evidence separate.
Why does the compact model miss recovery?
Its 781-bit pool keeps all operations and even the spare-furnace assignment rows. It excludes other coordinated choices needed for an on-time plan. Solving that pool exactly cannot restore a schedule outside its domain. Deterministic enrichment restores the optimum with 2,078 bits.
What does the quantum experiment contribute?
Actual simulated QAOA recombines complete component routes while retaining all 32 operations. It reaches the restricted optimum in all 18 saved runs, as does annealing. The classical constructor already supplies the best incumbent; no practical quantum advantage is established.
Why not use the 818-bit formulation for everything?
It asks an exact hard-deadline feasibility question. It does not preserve the original weighted-tardiness and completion objective. A checked zero-energy state proves feasibility; a nonzero heuristic sample does not prove impossibility.
Why not just use CP-SAT?
For these small synthetic queries, native CP is fast and a strong practical baseline. Its independent certificates let us distinguish solver failures from choices lost in the representation.
What do 18–36 qubits mean?
Logical circuit qubits, including completion choices, for restricted component-route models. Sector simulation enumerates energies classically and does not reduce the logical register. Six 18-qubit circuits receive independent full-Hilbert checks; larger circuits receive logical resource audits.
Are the costs and the factory real?
The planning inputs and prices are synthetic. The ledger values checked saved plans under explicit assumptions; it does not optimize money or establish calibrated industrial ROI. Final inspection completion is not physical shipment.
Where does VeloxQ fit?
The larger deadline, enriched and complete QUBOs are prepared offline. Actual service quality, runtime and cost remain unmeasured. No VeloxQ result is implied by these schedules.
