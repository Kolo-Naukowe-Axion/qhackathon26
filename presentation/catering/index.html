      Orders, batch choices and constraints

      In the small cell, one shared side batch completes two orders: the main already cooking for A, and a new main for B. Skip that side batch and both meals disappear from service, leaving two cooked mains to discard.

      Choose batch sizes, shared allocations, cookers, workers and start times on a 10-minute grid. A meal needs one main course and one side course, both packed before its fixed departure.

      Equipment and qualified workers cannot overlap. Freshness, calendars, prep, attended cooking, packing and cleaning constrain the plan. Started work stays committed. An outage also requires a worker to clean the repaired cooker before reuse.

      Paired-course objective

      For meal d, Md and Sd are 0/1 coverage expressions for its main and side, including committed food.

      U = ∑d pd(1 − MdSd)
      W = surplus + ∑d(Md + Sd − 2MdSd)

      One course alone produces waste. Pairing both serves the meal. Expanding these expressions gives quadratic coefficients with no extra product qubits.

      C(x) = wUU + wWW + wBB + wRR

      Weights encode the declared priority order: each weight exceeds the maximum sum of lower-priority costs.

      E(x) = C(x) + P ∑g(∑i∈gxi − 1)²
      + P ∑(i,j)∈conflictsxixj

      Every anchor block contains batch programs and an idle choice. P = 1 + the sum of absolute nonconstant objective coefficients, which exceeds the objective range. The all-idle continuation is feasible; therefore a ground state has zero hard-constraint penalty.

      QAOA applies cost phases and XY rotations within blocks. This preserves one-hot choices. Cross-block conflicts remain penalized and independently checked. Under x = (1 − Z)/2, the exported Ising coefficients represent exactly 4E.
